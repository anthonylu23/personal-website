I spent the last few months mostly listening to rock and indie. Not intentionally — it just happened. Playlists drifted that way, and I didn't think much of it.

Then a couple weeks ago I put on Brahms's Third Symphony again, and it hit differently. The second movement brought this sense of peace during what's been a chaotic start to the semester. There's a patience to it I don't think I fully appreciated before — maybe I needed the contrast of shorter, punchier music to hear it that way.

I've been working through the symphonies again since then. The First has a weight to its opening that I keep coming back to. The Second has this warmth that feels effortless but isn't. The Third is probably still my favorite — the way it ends quietly instead of with the big finale you expect feels like a deliberate choice to leave you thinking rather than applauding.

The violin and piano sonatas have been the real rediscovery though. They help me clear my thoughts in a way nothing else really does. The first sonata in G major has this simplicity that's deceptive — it sounds effortless, but there's so much going on underneath. The third sonata is the opposite, all intensity and restlessness. I've been alternating between the two and they almost feel like different composers.

Sometimes you need to step away from something to hear it again.
